\begin{abstract}
Generative image models make visual material for educational games cheap to produce, yet an image that looks right can still teach the wrong concept, such as one fourth drawn as one of four unequal parts. Common evaluators score aesthetics or text-image similarity, and vision-language model judges return holistic verdicts without checkable evidence. We present EduAssetVerify, which represents a learning objective as formal constraints, asks a vision-language model only for visual evidence, measures geometry deterministically, and lets rules decide each constraint, answering Uncertain when the evidence is insufficient. To evaluate verifiers without human raters, we build a counterfactual benchmark of fraction, counting, and spatial assets in which every correct asset is paired with a minimally altered violation and every label is checked against the rendered pixels. On 900 test images with two model families, EduAssetVerify accepted no violated asset, whereas direct, chain-of-thought, and structured judges accepted 0.6 to 4.3 percent, at the price of sending 1.5 to 3.1 percent of assets to review and four to nine times the judges' cost.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
generative AI, multimodal verification, educational game assets, vision-language models, constraint satisfaction, counterfactual evaluation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Generative artificial intelligence is being adopted across education, from tutoring dialogue to the creation of learning materials~\cite{kasneci2023chatgpt}. In educational games, text-to-image models make the many small visual assets a lesson needs, such as fraction tiles, counting cards, and left--right puzzles, cheap to produce. Unlike decorative art, these assets carry the concept being taught: visual design is one of the elements through which games support learning~\cite{plass2015foundations}, and a unit fraction $1/b$ is one of $b$ \emph{equal} parts of a whole~\cite{ccss2010math}, so a diagram with unequal parts misrepresents a concept that many learners already find difficult~\cite{siegler2013fractions}.

Such errors are hard to catch because the image remains plausible. CLIPScore~\cite{hessel2021clipscore} measures overall image--text compatibility, and CLIP-style models are largely insensitive to exact counts~\cite{paiss2023count}. Vision-language models (VLMs) used as judges~\cite{zheng2023judging,chen2024mllmjudge} miscount and misjudge elementary geometry~\cite{rahmanzadehgervi2024blind}, confuse spatial relations~\cite{kamath2023whatsup}, and report absent objects~\cite{li2023pope}, and their holistic verdicts carry no evidence that a teacher could inspect. We are not aware of a controlled benchmark with exact ground truth for educational concept violations.

Experience with generated game assets shows how easily model-judged evaluation misleads~\cite{anon2026legibility}. Scores of a single judge shifted between identical scoring passes by as much as the effect under study, a deprecated request parameter made one judge fail on every call, which a naive harness would have scored as wrong answers, and rankings did not survive a change of judge. Conclusions became trustworthy only with an executable answer key and judges that passed validity checks before scoring. Educational assets suit these lessons, because the answer key can be generated together with the asset.

We therefore ask whether constraint-grounded multimodal verification can reliably detect educational concept violations in AI-generated visual assets without human evaluation. This requires:
\begin{enumerate}
\item turning an abstract learning objective into constraints that a machine can check;
\item extracting verifiable visual evidence from an image instead of trusting a VLM's overall verdict; and
\item detecting a local violation without being swayed by style or background.
\end{enumerate}
We propose EduAssetVerify, which separates perception from judgment: a VLM reports what the image contains without being told what the constraints expect, deterministic measurement and rules decide each formal constraint, and a VLM semantic verifier handles the constraints that resist formalization. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a constraint schema for educational visual assets, with a consistency check;
\item a verifier that combines value-blind VLM grounding, deterministic measurement, and three-valued rules with abstention; and
\item a counterfactual benchmark with programmatically verified labels, evaluated under a protocol that guards against known failure modes of model judges.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Generative AI for Educational Visual Content}
Generative tools are being adopted to create learning materials, alongside risks of inaccurate content~\cite{kasneci2023chatgpt}. Text-guided systems produce scientific figures as code~\cite{belouadi2024automatikz} and game assets conditioned on gameplay structure~\cite{anon2026legibility}, yet quality is still assessed with general image-quality and alignment scores that do not encode the instructional rule an asset must obey.

\subsection{Vision-Language Models for Visual Verification}
Model judges grade generated content at scale~\cite{zheng2023judging}, and multimodal judges extend the practice to images, with documented biases~\cite{chen2024mllmjudge}. Text-to-image evaluation decomposes a prompt into questions~\cite{cho2024dsg} or checks counts and positions with detectors~\cite{ghosh2023geneval}, but each check still ends in a model's answer. EduAssetVerify uses the VLM to extract evidence, not to deliver the verdict.

\subsection{Constraint-Based and Neuro-Symbolic Verification}
Neuro-symbolic visual question answering executes symbolic programs on parsed scenes~\cite{yi2018nsvqa}, and visual programming composes perception modules through generated code~\cite{gupta2023visprog,suris2023vipergpt}. EduAssetVerify adopts this parse-then-check structure to decide whether an image satisfies an explicit educational constraint set, with \Uncertain{} as an admissible answer.

\subsection{Counterfactual and Selective Evaluation}
Procedurally rendered scenes with exact ground truth enable diagnostic evaluation~\cite{johnson2017clevr}, contrast sets test minimally edited inputs~\cite{gardner2020contrast}, and uncontrolled benchmarks can be solved through shortcuts~\cite{hsieh2023sugarcrepe}. Metamorphic testing relates the outputs of transformed inputs~\cite{chen2018metamorphic}, and selective prediction lets a model abstain to lower its risk~\cite{geifman2017selective,whitehead2022reliable}. Our benchmark combines exact oracles, single-property violations, and style changes that must leave the verdict unchanged.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:formulation}

A learning objective $L$, such as ``represent one fourth,'' induces a constraint set $C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_n\}$ that every image $I$ teaching $L$ must satisfy. A verifier computes
\begin{equation}
V(I,C)\in\{\Valid,\Invalid,\Uncertain\},\label{eq:task}
\end{equation}
where \Uncertain{} routes the asset to a human reviewer. Beyond deciding $V$, a verifier should name the violated constraint and the affected region, and remain correct across visual styles, asset templates, and error types.

Each constraint names a subject, a predicate, an operator, a target value, and, for measured quantities, a tolerance. The schema has 15 constraint names of four types: cardinality (counts), geometric (areas and symmetry), spatial (relative position and orientation), and semantic (categories and labels). For one fourth shown as a divided whole,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{1/4}=\{\,&\cn{part\_count}=4,\ \cn{equal\_area},\\
&\cn{colored\_part\_count}=1,\\
&\cn{fraction\_value}=0.25\,\}.
\end{aligned}\label{eq:example}
\end{equation}
The last constraint follows from the others; stating it lets a consistency check reject an objective such as ``one third, shown as one of four equal parts'' before any image is verified.

\subsection{Constraint Parser}\label{sec:parser}
A language model, the same backbone as the verifier, transcribes every requirement stated in $L$ into the schema and keeps conflicting requirements rather than resolving them. A deterministic check then rejects unsatisfiable sets, such as conflicting values, a fraction or label that disagrees with the part counts, or cyclic spatial relations. Gold constraints, authored with each template, are evaluated separately from extracted ones, so that an extraction error is never counted as an image-verification error.

\subsection{Value-Blind Grounding and Measurement}\label{sec:grounding}
A VLM reads the image into an evidence graph $G_I=(O_I,R_I,A_I)$ of objects and regions with boxes, their relations, and attributes such as color and text. The prompt names the vocabulary of $C$ (for example, \emph{part}, \emph{colored}, \emph{apple}) but no target values, because a model asked to confirm four equal parts can agree without looking. Objects are listed rather than confirmed, with categories from the whole domain vocabulary. Because VLMs misjudge precise geometry~\cite{rahmanzadehgervi2024blind}, deterministic operators measure it without knowledge of the palette: a divided shape is segmented into the regions bounded by color edges inside its reported box, objects are counted as foreground components, and mirror overlap and arrow orientation are computed from the foreground mask. Grounding is repeated $m=3$ times.

\subsection{Hybrid Verification and Abstention}\label{sec:verify}
Each constraint $c_j$ receives a status $s_j\in\{\sT,\sF,\sU\}$. Rules decide the formal constraints: a measured deviation $d_j$ receives
\begin{equation}
s_j=\sT \text{ if } d_j\le\epsilon_j^{\sT},\quad \sF \text{ if } d_j\ge\epsilon_j^{\sF},\quad \sU \text{ otherwise},\label{eq:zones}
\end{equation}
and a failed precondition, such as parts that cannot be segmented, gives $\sU$. Semantic constraints are posed as open questions about nodes of $G_I$: labels are re-read from a crop, categories from a numbered montage of the grounded objects, and the rules compare the answers with the targets. The confidence $q_j$ averages the agreement of the $m$ readings with the majority status, weighting measured readings by their distance to the nearest threshold of~\eqref{eq:zones}, and a status with $q_j<\tau$ becomes $\sU$. With $\sF<\sU<\sT$, the verdict is the strong Kleene conjunction
\begin{equation}
V(I,C)=\begin{cases}\Valid, & \min_j s_j=\sT,\\ \Invalid, & \min_j s_j=\sF,\\ \Uncertain, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}\label{eq:verdict}
\end{equation}
so one well-evidenced violation rejects an asset, whereas acceptance requires every constraint to be verified. The report lists each status with its evidence, measured values, and witness region, such as the part whose area deviates most.

\section{Counterfactual Educational Asset Benchmark}\label{sec:benchmark}

The benchmark covers three tasks with five templates each, three for development and two held out: fraction understanding (circles, strips, and grids, optionally labeled), object counting and classification (exact counts, distractors, two groups, optional numeral), and spatial reasoning (left--right, above--below, three-object order, mirror symmetry, and quarter-turn rotation). Each violated asset $I^{-}=T_k(I^{+})$ applies one of nine operators to a correct asset, covering seven error types: missing object, extra object, incorrect proportion, incorrect label, spatial relation reversal, broken symmetry, and semantic mismatch. Operators act on the scene specification, and an assertion checks that exactly the targeted constraint changes status. A procedural renderer draws each scene at $512\times512$ pixels in one of seven styles, and before any verifier runs, an independent script recomputes every label from the pixels; an item is kept only if both answers agree~\cite{anon2026legibility}. The benchmark has 1{,}020 images: 120 for development, which fixes prompts and thresholds, and 900 for testing, 300 per task, under four conditions: in-domain, unseen templates, combinations of two operators, and four unseen styles.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

We ask whether EduAssetVerify detects violations better than VLM judges (RQ1), localizes them (RQ2), generalizes to unseen conditions (RQ3), and at what cost (RQ4).

\emph{Baselines.} CLIPScore~\cite{hessel2021clipscore} with ViT-B/32, thresholded on the development split; a VLM direct judge; a chain-of-thought (CoT) judge; a structured judge that checks each constraint in turn; and a rule-only validator that uses our evidence without the semantic verifier and abstention, mapping $\sU$ to \Invalid. All VLM methods receive the same images, objective, and constraints.

\emph{Metrics.} With \Invalid{} as the positive class, the false acceptance rate (FAR) is the fraction of violated assets judged \Valid{} and the false rejection rate (FRR) the fraction of correct assets judged \Invalid; abstention counts against accuracy but never as acceptance. A witness localizes correctly at an intersection over union of at least 0.5 with a perturbed region.

\emph{Protocol.} Before scoring, each backbone passed a five-check validity battery~\cite{anon2026legibility} as grounder and as judge: protocol compliance, test--retest agreement, permutation invariance, a blank-image control, and accuracy above a majority guess. On the development split, the battery revealed that Claude answered boxes in pixels although asked for normalized units, so each model is asked for boxes in its native coordinate frame. Every configuration ran three independent passes; we report means and claim a difference only if it exceeds two standard errors under both backbones.

\emph{Models and settings.} \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash} (thinking budget 1{,}024 tokens, temperature 0.7) and Claude Sonnet~5 through Amazon Bedrock (default sampling), each also serving as parser; $m=3$, $\tau=0.6$, equal-area zones $(0.10, 0.20)$, and mirror-overlap zones $(0.92, 0.82)$, all fixed on the development split.

\emph{Detection (RQ1).} EduAssetVerify accepted no violated asset under either backbone, 0 of 1{,}350 violated evaluations each, whereas the judges accepted 8 to 9 under Gemini and 42 to 58 under Claude (Table~\ref{tab:main}); the difference exceeds two standard errors for every judge. The zero costs abstention on 3.1\% and 1.5\% of assets. Counting abstentions as errors, its accuracy is below the judges' under Gemini (96.7\% against 99.1 to 99.5\%) and above it under Claude (98.5\% against 95.7 to 97.7\%). The rule-only validator also accepts no violation and is the most accurate method, and CLIPScore is at chance. The judges' false acceptances concentrate where correctness is a measured quantity: under Claude, the direct judge accepted 22\% of unequal partitions, 14\% of wrong part counts, and 12\% of broken symmetries, while counting errors were almost always caught.

\emph{Generalization (RQ3).} The FAR of EduAssetVerify stayed at zero in every test condition, whereas the judges' reached 6.7\% (Claude, unseen styles). Abstentions cost pairs: under Gemini, the fraction of pairs with both images judged correctly was 1.4 to 5.8 points below the judges' in every condition, whereas under Claude it exceeded both judges in-domain and on unseen templates (98.5\% and 97.6\%) and stayed within one point of the CoT judge elsewhere. Unseen styles were the hardest condition for the judges; for EduAssetVerify, a hand-drawn wobble breaks exact mirror symmetry, and such figures were sent to review.

\emph{Ablation and localization (RQ2).} Measurement matters most (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}): without it, Claude's FAR rises to 1.9\% and its FRR to 8.3\%. Letting the VLM apply the rules to the same evidence raises FRR to 1.6\% and 3.8\%. The semantic verifier shows no measurable benefit, and abstention prevents no false acceptance; it turns false rejections into reviews. Over correct rejections of localizable errors, the reported violations are perturbed constraints in 99.6\% and 100\% of cases, and the witness hits the perturbed region in 94.2\% and 85.5\%. The misses come from symmetry, where the moved element and its partner are indistinguishable, and from text boxes smaller than the ground truth.

\emph{Extracted constraints.} With parser-produced constraints, the pipeline accepted 2 of 1{,}350 violated evaluations under Gemini and none under Claude (Table~\ref{tab:main}, last row). The parsers reproduced the gold set for 83.8\% and 79.0\% of objectives, and both false acceptances trace to parses that dropped ``and nothing else.'' The consistency check rejected 88.9\% and 100\% of 60 contradictory objectives without false alarms. A first run of this regime on the test split exposed a vocabulary mismatch, the divided whole being parsed as an object that must be present; we corrected the parser instruction on the development split and report the second run.

\emph{Cost (RQ4).} EduAssetVerify makes 4.9 VLM calls per asset, three readings plus re-readings, with 12 to 16 s of summed latency, and costs 4.2 to 9.2 times as much as a single judge call: USD 4.62 and 25.16 per 1{,}000 assets, against 0.84 to 1.11 and 2.73 to 5.50, at assumed token rates. Under Gemini, 54 of the 84 abstentions followed a malformed reply, which occurred in 2.2\% of readings.


\emph{Qualitative analysis.} Fig.~\ref{fig:qual} shows one case of each kind. All three judges accepted the unequal partition, in which one uncolored part deviates from an equal share by 61\% while the colored parts keep their exact share, the misconception the operator models. The false rejection arose because the crop around a correctly grounded label also contained the circle's outline, and two of three re-readings returned ``)5/8''; the sketch-style figure was sent to review because its mirror overlap of 0.88 lies between the two zones.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

EduAssetVerify can gate automated material production, with its abstentions as the review queue for teachers or designers. Because its advantage over the judges lies in measured proportions and symmetries, the semantic verifier and abstention, which we expected to matter, are optional on assets like ours: the rule-only configuration was as safe, more accurate, and cheaper.

We do not measure learning outcomes, and only concepts expressible as constraints are covered. The benchmark is procedurally rendered: we built no cross-generator condition, because generator outputs lack labels that can be confirmed without raters. Under Gemini, the judges are near ceiling, which leaves little room to separate methods on accuracy, and costs rest on assumed token prices. Future work includes harder concepts and diagrams, labeled generator outputs, automatic repair from the violation report, and studies with teachers.

\section{Conclusion}
EduAssetVerify verifies AI-generated educational assets by grounding a VLM's evidence in formal constraints, deterministic measurement, and three-valued rules. On a counterfactual benchmark with verified labels, it accepted no violated asset under two model families, whereas VLM judges accepted up to 4.3\%, at the price of reviewing 1.5 to 3.1\% of assets and a four- to ninefold cost. Its value comes from measured, value-blind evidence; claims about learning outcomes require studies with learners.
